As the Diabetic Eye Screening (DES) service continues to develop, the programme is working with suppliers, operational and clinical teams, quality assurance (QA) colleagues and pilot sites to test the new approach to DES Cohorting.

The pilot provides an opportunity to understand how the new process works in practice, assess its operational and clinical impacts, and identify any changes needed before progressing through the next phases.

Regular engagement and structured feedback bring together the teams involved in delivering the service, helping the programme review progress, address issues and make informed decisions as the pilot develops.


## Working collaboratively with suppliers and pilot sites

The DES Cohorting pilot involves collaboration across operational delivery, Clinical Safety teams, QA, suppliers and pilot site stakeholders.

Each group brings a different perspective. Operational teams can identify practical implications, clinical colleagues can assess potential impacts on screening delivery, and QA colleagues can consider how the process and its outputs should be reviewed and assured.

Suppliers and pilot sites provide insight into how cohorting operates in practice, including how information is handled and where further guidance or clarification may be needed.

Bringing these perspectives together allows the programme to identify potential issues early, understand their implications and agree appropriate actions before progressing to subsequent pilot phases.


## Establishing regular engagement through the CASCADE Operational Working Group


To support this collaborative approach, the programme has established regular CASCADE Operational Working Group calls. CASCADE (CaaS Cohorting and Diabetic Eye) brings together operational, clinical, QA and pilot site stakeholders to review pilot progress, discuss operational and clinical impacts, and share insights to support national programme decision-making.

The rolling agenda covers four key areas:
-	Pilot delivery update: Reviewing progress, current activities and developments across the pilot.
-	Cohort comparison update: Sharing findings and discussing their implications for the new process.
-	Pilot successes, risks and issues: Identifying what is working well, discussing concerns and agreeing where further investigation or action is required.
-	Programme guidance and policy needs: Identifying where additional guidance, clarification or policy decisions may be needed to support consistent delivery.

Having a consistent agenda helps maintain focus while allowing emerging issues to be discussed. It also provides an opportunity to consider different stakeholder perspectives and understand the wider implications of decisions across the service.


## Supporting day-to-day delivery through daily pilot check-ins

Alongside the working group, the programme is holding daily DES Cascade Pilot check-ins.

These shorter sessions provide an opportunity to share updates, raise emerging issues and coordinate actions to support day-to-day pilot delivery.

While the Operational Working Group focuses on reviewing progress, considering wider impacts and informing decisions about the pilot phases, daily check-ins support ongoing coordination.

This allows issues to be raised and investigated as they emerge. Where a matter requires broader discussion, further evidence or input from additional stakeholders, it can be taken to the appropriate forum.

Together, these meetings support communication, issue management and decision-making throughout the pilot.


## Using cohort comparisons to understand the impact of the new process

Reviewing cohort comparisons is an important part of understanding how the new DES Cohorting process performs in practice.

The findings are considered alongside operational and clinical feedback, allowing the programme to assess not only what the comparisons show but also what they may mean for service delivery.

This combined approach helps identify areas requiring further investigation, clarification or a decision before the next pilot phase can progress.

Cohort comparison updates therefore contribute to the ongoing assessment of the new process, informing potential refinements to the cascade process and supporting decisions about readiness for national rollout.


## Identifying and managing risks, issues and dependencies

Testing a new process in a live service context requires the programme to understand where risks and issues may arise and how they should be managed.

Concerns may relate to the practical operation of the process, potential clinical or operational impacts, interpretation of requirements, or the need for additional programme guidance or policy decisions.

Daily check-ins provide an opportunity to raise emerging concerns and coordinate activity, while the Operational Working Group supports wider discussion, review and agreement of next steps.

Where further work is needed, the programme can work with relevant suppliers, pilot sites and internal teams to understand the issue, gather additional information and determine the appropriate response.

This helps ensure that decisions are informed by evidence and that potential impacts are understood before changes are taken forward.


## Using feedback to inform the next pilot phases

Feedback from suppliers, pilot sites and operational, clinical and QA stakeholders is considered alongside cohort comparison findings and the risks and issues identified during delivery.

Bringing this information together gives the programme a broader understanding of how the new process operates in practice and what needs to be considered as it develops.

The CASCADE Operational Working Group provides a forum for discussing findings and informing decisions about the pilot phases, while daily check-ins support ongoing communication and coordination.

This allows learning from the pilot to inform the next steps, rather than relying solely on assumptions about how the new process will operate. It also supports a more controlled progression through the pilot phases, with decisions based on available evidence, feedback and an understanding of operational and clinical impacts.


## Next steps

The programme will continue working with suppliers, pilot sites, operational and clinical teams, and QA colleagues through daily DES Cascade Pilot check-ins and CASCADE Operational Working Group calls.

The immediate focus is to maintain visibility of pilot delivery, review cohort comparison updates and identify any operational or clinical impacts requiring further consideration.

The programme will also continue to review successes, risks and issues, ensuring outstanding questions are discussed with the appropriate stakeholders and actions are followed through.

Where additional guidance, clarification or policy decisions are needed, these will be raised through the relevant discussions to understand their implications for pilot delivery and subsequent phases.

As the pilot progresses, the learning gathered through these activities will help inform decisions about the next phases of the new DES Cohorting process.
