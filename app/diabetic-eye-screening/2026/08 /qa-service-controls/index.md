As the Diabetic Eye Screening (DES) service continues to develop, the programme has been looking at how quality assurance (QA) is currently delivered across the service and how existing controls can support the future service model.

The aim of this work is to build a clear understanding of the QA processes, controls and measures already in place across the service. This includes understanding how controls operate in practice, where assurance is currently provided, who owns different activities, and whether existing arrangements continue to meet the requirements of the future service.

The work is not about creating a new QA approach from scratch. Instead, the programme is building on the controls and measures that already exist, identifying where they provide the required assurance and focusing effort on areas where additional requirements, changes or strengthening may be needed.

## Working collaboratively to understand existing controls

The QA discovery has been carried out collaboratively with teams involved in delivering, managing and assuring the existing service.

The programme has held workshops involving the Service Management team, GP2DRS team, Mike Olson (our Quality Assurance Analyst from Health Information Consulting (HIC)), third-party suppliers, and other internal teams.

These workshops have provided an opportunity to understand how QA currently works across different parts of the service, including the controls used to identify issues, validate information, monitor processing and support operational decision-making.

Working directly with the teams responsible for these processes has been important in understanding not just what controls are documented, but how they operate in practice.

The collaborative approach has also helped the programme test assumptions about what may be required in the future service. In some cases, an apparent gap has been found to have an existing control that already provides the required level of assurance.

This means the programme can focus on genuine areas for improvement rather than introducing new processes where effective controls already exist.


## Bringing the findings together in a QA Matrix

The findings from the workshops are being brought together into a single QA Matrix.

The Matrix provides a structured way of comparing programme requirements with the controls and measures currently operating across the service. It allows the team to understand where assurance is already being provided and where further work may be required.

The Matrix is being used to identify:

-	existing controls and measures currently operating across the service
-	areas where existing controls already meet programme requirements
-	potential gaps where further assurance may be needed
-	areas where existing processes may need to be adapted or strengthened
-	questions that require further investigation or clarification
-	actions and ownership where changes are identified

Bringing the findings together in this way gives the programme a consistent view of QA across the service and provides a clear evidence base for discussions with the teams responsible for each control.


## Building on what already works

One of the key findings from the work so far is that a number of existing controls and measures already provide the assurance required by the programme.

Existing technical and operational processes provide assurance across a range of areas, including data validation, monitoring, processing, exception handling and reconciliation.

These controls provide an important foundation for the future service.

The discovery work has therefore reinforced that the programme is not starting from scratch with QA. There is already significant assurance embedded within the existing service. The focus is on understanding how these controls work, whether they remain appropriate for the future service, and how they should be owned and operated going forward.

This approach also helps avoid unnecessary duplication. Where an existing control provides the required assurance, the intention is to retain and build on it rather than introduce an additional process solely to satisfy a requirement.
 
 
## Identifying where further work is needed

While the discovery has identified many effective existing controls, it has also highlighted areas where further work may be required.

The team is working through these areas to understand whether there is a genuine gap, whether an existing control needs to be strengthened, or whether further clarification is needed before a decision can be made.

As part of this, the programme is also considering where QA activities can take place as early as possible in the process, so that data quality issues can be identified and addressed at or close to their source. At the same time, the programme is seeking to avoid unnecessarily reworking or duplicating QA processes that are already effective, simply to introduce a new approach.

A particular area of consideration is the relationship between controls, ownership and escalation.

A control may already exist technically, for example through monitoring or reporting, but the future service also needs to be clear about who is responsible for reviewing the output, responding to exceptions and escalating issues where necessary.

The same principle applies to existing operational processes. Where a process is currently dependent on a particular team, supplier or way of working, the programme is considering whether that arrangement remains appropriate for the future service or whether ownership needs to be clarified or transferred.

The objective is therefore not to change every existing process. Instead, each requirement is being assessed against the current service to determine what, if anything, needs to change.


## Presenting findings and agreeing actions

As the QA Matrix is finalised, findings will be presented back to the relevant teams in a consistent format.

Where a potential gap or concern has been identified, the analysis will set out:

-	problem statement: what assurance concern or risk has been identified
-	existing control:  what is already in place to manage the requirement or risk
-	detail of the concern: why the existing arrangement may not provide sufficient assurance, or why further clarification is needed
-	RAG status: the current assessment of the level of concern
-	proposed solution: the change, clarification or additional control being considered
-	ownership: which team or role will be responsible for taking the action forward
-	outstanding questions: information still required before the position can be confirmed

This provides a clear way for teams to understand the finding, the evidence behind it and what is being proposed.

It also gives the relevant teams an opportunity to challenge or validate the assessment before any changes are agreed.


## Key outcomes from the discovery work

The QA discovery has enabled the programme to:

-	build a clearer understanding of existing QA processes, controls and measures across the DES service
-	bring together knowledge from Service Management, GP2DRS, QA, supplier and internal teams
-	identify existing controls that already provide the required level of assurance
-	avoid unnecessarily redesigning or duplicating effective existing processes
-	identify potential gaps and areas where further investigation is required
-	highlight where existing processes, ownership or escalation arrangements may need to be strengthened
-	establish a QA Matrix as a shared view of the current findings
-	create a consistent approach for presenting concerns and agreeing potential solutions


## Next steps

The programme will continue working with the Service Management team, the GP2DRS team and Mike Olson, our Quality Assurance Analyst from Health Information Consulting (HIC), as well as third-party suppliers and other internal teams, to complete the remaining analysis.

The immediate next steps are to finalise the QA Matrix, resolve outstanding questions and confirm the position for areas where further investigation is required.

Where genuine gaps or opportunities to strengthen existing processes have been identified, the programme will work with the relevant teams to define the requirements and agree the appropriate solution, including ownership and any changes required to existing controls.

Findings will then be presented back to the relevant teams using the agreed approach of problem statement, RAG status, detail of the concern and proposed solution.
 

## Supporting the future service model

The QA discovery provides an important foundation for the future DES service.

By understanding and building on the controls that already exist, the programme can retain effective assurance while focusing effort on the areas where change is genuinely needed.

The work will help ensure that QA remains proportionate, consistent and clearly owned as the service evolves, while avoiding unnecessary duplication or introducing new processes where existing controls already provide the required assurance.

The approach also provides a repeatable way of assessing QA as further elements of the service develop, building on the knowledge and capability of the teams who operate and assure the service.

Ultimately, the aim is to move towards a future service model where the right controls are in place, their purpose is understood, ownership is clear, and potential issues can be identified and addressed effectively.
