The Breast Screening Trust Engagement Team’s remit is to be the link between NHS Trusts and the NHS England teams building the replacement for the National Breast Screening System (NBSS).
As well as telling them about the planned changes around the move from NBSS to Rubie (Run Breast Screening in England), we’re also gathering organisational, operational, and technical intelligence about breast screening providers. This means we can monitor readiness, identify any risks, and help navigate the governance underpinning NBSS data extraction and future roll-out of Rubie to providers.




## How to contact Trusts: Finding a way in
To find the people we needed to speak to in the Trusts (initially information governance (IG) folks) we tried a few routes: 
•	used our NHS network – a former colleague had compiled a list of Trust Data Protection Officers and Information Governance colleagues for another NHSE national programme, and kindly shared this with us
•	we contacted Breast Screening Offices (BSOs) directly to ask if they had contacts in their Trust they could share
•	the national Digital Partnering Team were really helpful in telling Trusts CDIOs about this work and finding any missing contacts

### The first conversations
Once we had found people to talk to, we set up initial meetings. In these early discussions, we gave a presentation designed to introduce the Rubie programme: outlining benefits, explaining the data transformation approach, and setting out the proposed timelines.
We wanted to better understand each Trust's information governance and data access processes (often called the ‘front door’ process). Also, we needed to find relevant IT and technical colleagues who could support the next phase of engagement (which focuses on identifying NBSS database location, size, management, and potential options for extracting data to be sent to NHSE for data transformation work).  




## What we’ve learned so far:
### Build relationships early: 
Since starting this work in May 2026, we’ve spoken with Information Governance (IG) teams in 51 Trusts (of 73) and facilitated conversations with 26 technical teams within those Trusts. We’re beginning to understand how and where NBSS databases sit within Trusts and are in a good place to start with data extraction as soon as we have the green light from our IG colleagues and signed Data Protection Impact Assessments (DPIAs) and Data Sharing Arrangements (DSAs) in place. By building these relationships early through meetings and follow-ups, we’re ahead of the game on data extraction but also laying the groundwork for working with Trusts through the wider Rubie roll-out, establishing gateways between the new system and Trust infrastructure.

### Take a one-to-one approach: 
As each Trust currently operates its own instance of NBSS, a one-to-one engagement approach has been valuable to establish strong, tailored relationships and develop a detailed understanding of how individual Trusts operate. This includes understanding their local processes, upcoming workplans, requirements and ways of working. It has also allowed Trusts to have an open safe space to clarify what is needed from them and highlight any questions or concerns.

### Track appetite to inform roll-out planning:
We’ve been gathering an initial snapshot of appetite across Trusts for change, including capacity to engage with our programme (for example in terms of existing workplans priorities and workforce availability). This can help inform Rubie roll-out planning by indicating interest and barriers to engagement, including identifying who may be best placed to be early adopters or fast followers. 

### Regularly update comms and engagement material:
As our calls progressed, we reflected regularly on our presentation materials to refine the content based on conversations held to improve the flow and effectiveness of calls. We noted questions raised during calls and amended slide content to address these key themes and queries from the outset. We simplified language, reduced technical detail and put more emphasis and a focus on programme outcomes, benefits, and practical implications for Trusts. 

### Collate a Frequently Asked Questions list: 
We have noted and tracked all new questions arising in calls and through our inbox, contacting colleagues in the wider programme to give answers or 'lines to take' to ensure consistency. We hold a monthly ‘FAQ review’ session to enable discussion around new questions with cross-team colleagues. It’s been really useful to refer to this list to swiftly answer similar questions in calls or emails, and we have generated a list of these frequently (and sometimes infrequently) asked questions that are published on [NHS Futures](Rubie / NBSS Replacement FAQs - Vaccinations and Screening - Futures)

### Get networking across the NHS: 
NHS contact details across the system are not widely or transparently available, which can make it difficult to identify target contacts. To help us seek Trust IG contacts, we leveraged our cross-NHS relationships to reach out to another NHSE national programme who already held a similar stakeholder list. 

### Map how Trusts work with each other: 
We have started to build a picture of which Trusts work together on information governance, corporate services and/or technical and IT support. This has been useful to link up conversations with these Trusts to provide an aligned message but also hold calls with co-working Trusts together.  




> In summary, our key learning has been the value of an early, tailored, one-to-one approach, particularly given the differences in how Trusts operate and manage their NBSS instances. 
Building strong relationships is fundamental to the successful rollout of Rubie as NHS Trusts play a key role in enabling implementation of the new system and supporting its integration across Breast Screening Offices (BSOs). A collaborative approach will help support a smoother, more effective and safer transition from NBSS to Rubie. 

> Whilst Rubie is a national programme, we believe that success will be determined locally.
