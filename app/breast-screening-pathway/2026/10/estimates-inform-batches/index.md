In a recent round of interviews, we deepened our understanding of how breast screening offices (BSOs) select participants for screening. This process is known as batching.

Making sure no one is missed is a key goal for every BSO. 

Longer-term population estimates set a target that shorter-term batches are checked against. These can be done by different people within the same BSO and checking each other’s work acts as a failsafe. 

## Round plans, estimates and batches

BSOs plan across 3 horizons:

- round plan: typically looks 3 years ahead
- estimate batches: look 1 year ahead and are based on annual population figures
- specify batches: look 6 to 8 weeks ahead and specify who will be invited

The round plan is adjusted based on estimates and round length. Estimating is a yearly activity, whereas batching is a more frequent activity, with BSOs telling us they specify 1 to 3 batches a week. 

## Comparing estimates with batches is a safety check

Comparing batch sizes with estimate sizes acts as a safety check. If the numbers match within tens of people, BSOs know they are on track.

If the numbers are out by 100 or more, they know something is off. Staff interrogate the figures and check for errors or GP practice mergers.

## Batching is often shared between 2 people

One staff member prepares the yearly estimate once the annual population figures come through. These estimates are often done in spreadsheets. 

Another staff member takes those estimates to 'specify the batches' in BS Select.

The people working together are often a manager and their deputy: for example, a programme manager and deputy, an admin lead and deputy or an office manager and deputy.

Their goal is to check each other's work, refine estimates, move towards more precise numbers, spot outliers and make sure no one is missed.

> [!NOTE]
> “I will do the specification, then run that past the admin lead. If she is happy with what I’ve estimated against her round plan of population estimates, we know it’s right. If it’s way out, then we review”

## BSOs do it differently

Over time, experienced staff develop a sense of what an accurate figure looks like. Each BSO has its own process and way of doing things. In London, each of the 6 BSOs sends its requirements to the hub, which specifies the batches on their behalf. 

> [!NOTE]
> "The documentation for how I do it is not robust, I’m not sure someone could pick this up. I learned by osmosis and there are lots of 'unless this…' or 'if this…'"

Some BSOs split their population by months of birth, and screen a third of their cohort once a year. 

> [!NOTE]
> "It's a 3-year screening round and we split it into 3 one-year batches by month of birth: those born from January to April, May to August and September to December"

## Small changes can have big implications

The usability testing showed that even small changes to batching screens on BS Select can affect policies, training and processes beyond what we had anticipated.

## What this means for Rubie

Rubie will need to support BSOs go from population estaimtes to batches and:

- enable safety checks
- help staff gain certainty and detect where numbers are out

Rubie teams will need decide which local variations to support and where to steer towards a simpler standardised process.
