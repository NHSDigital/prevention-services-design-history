Breast screening offices (BSOs) use the batching screens in BS Select to select participants for invitation. We proposed changes to the NTDD (next test due date) batching screen, including new controls for selecting age range and removing 2 elements we [believed](/breast-screening-select/2026/10/create-ntdd-batch/) were no longer needed.

In October 2026 we tested these changes with 8 BSOs. We wanted to learn whether:

- removing the “Include younger women…” checkbox and the “call or recall” field caused any problems
- the new controls for selecting age range met BSOs' needs
- participants noticed changes in the information box

![Old and new version](variants.png 'The existing page in BS Select (left) and the proposed design (right) that was created for testing')

## What we did

We ran 30-minute remote usability testing sessions. Some staff joined alone and some with colleagues from admin, programme management and radiology.

4 BSOs used NTDD and 4 used [RI/SP (recall interval / safety period)](/breast-screening-pathway/2025/12/understanding-batching/) as their main batching method. BSOs using RI/SP rarely see this screen, so most of what we learned came from NTDD BSOs.

## Removing the age radio buttons affects existing processes

The new design removed the “Age / Date of Birth” radio buttons. This confused 2 BSOs, who told us it would mean changing how they work. They estimate batches in Excel using dates of birth.

> [!NOTE]
> "We don't screen like that. We do it by date of birth"

Another BSO relied on the Helper tool to calculate the exact dates to enter:

> [!NOTE]
> "Would use the tool to work this out, to stop mistakes being made. Helper tool works out to the exact dates we need to put"

![NTDD screen on BS Select](batch-dob.png 'A BSO entered dates of birth, as this is how their current process works')

## The “Include younger women...” checkbox

4 BSOs used the “Include younger women…” checkbox. They had been trained to use it, or “had always done it this way”.

> [!NOTE]
> "It's something we've always done, management prior to me did this. That's the way I've been taught"

Participants believed the checkbox included people aged between 49 and 50. Their motivation was to make sure no one was missed.

No one commented on the removal of the “call or recall” field, which suggests it is safe to remove.

![BS Select screenshot](checkbox.png '"The Include younger women…” checkbox in BS Select')

## Participants missed the information box

Participants did not pause to read the information box at the top of the screen or comment on what it said.

BSO staff use the batching pages several times a week. They know what the page looks like, so they skip past what they expect to see.

## What this means for design

Future work should take into consideration the following recommendations.

- Keep the “Age / Date of Birth” radio buttons and understand more about how batching by date of birth is used in BSOs.
- Clarify the rules (policy) governing the use of the “Include younger women…” checkbox and assess the full impacts of removing it, focusing on making sure no one is missed.
- Do not rely on the information box alone. When something on the page changes, make the box look different or move it, so it’s closer to the element that changed.

## What's next

We have updated the prototype to keep the age and date of birth radio buttons. Jointly with the programme team, we will assess the impacts of the removal of the “Include younger women…” checkbox. If this is agreed, we will decide how to best communicate it to BSOs and other interested parties.

![Updated prototype](updated-prototype.png 'Updated prototype summarising proposed changes')

We also want to understand more about how BSOs use dates of birth and the Helper tool to build batches, and whether the service should do some of that work for them.

We thought that removing a checkbox and a pair of radio buttons were small changes, but they turned out to have implications beyond what we could have imagined without speaking to BSOs.
